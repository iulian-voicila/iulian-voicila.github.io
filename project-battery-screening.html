Research / Second-life battery screening

Doctoral research

Second-life battery screening

Intelligent sorting system for end-of-life Li-ion batteries from the automotive industry

Open datasets

Overview

Batteries retired from electric and hybrid vehicles usually keep around 80% of their initial capacity. Recycling recovers the materials, but many of these cells can still serve in applications with lower energy demands, provided their condition is known. Before a cell can be reused it has to be tested and sorted, and testing is slow.

My doctoral research proposed a sorting method and an automated system for this step. The method adds an intermediate pre-sorting stage that shortens the time needed to estimate the state of health. The system is built around a bidirectional (current and voltage) static power converter that tests two or more cells of different chemistries at the same time, and it is modular, so it can be extended to more cells or to battery modules and packs.

My role

  • Design of the multi-chemistry, multi-battery state-of-health screening system and its pre-sorting method.
  • Analysis of bidirectional DC-DC converter topologies for battery screening.
  • Statistical estimation of open-circuit voltage in LiFePO4 cells using knee/elbow detection.
  • Laboratory measurement campaigns, later released as two open datasets.
